## Market shape

Australian recruitment in early 2026 sits between two structural pressures:

1. **Macro cooling.** Hiring volumes are softer than 2023-24 peaks across tech, professional services, and white-collar generally. Some categories down 20-30% YoY.
2. **Senior talent scarcity.** Despite overall cooling, mid-to-senior placements remain hard. Passive candidates are slower to move; counter-offers are common.

Boutique agencies are picking up share from larger generalist firms — clients are paying premium fees for genuine vetting because the cost of a mis-hire at senior level outweighs the saved recruiter fee.

## Where Rallee's positioning fits

The market is bifurcating:
- **Volume / commodity recruiting** — Hays, Robert Half, generalist firms. Race-to-bottom on fees.
- **High-touch boutique** — small founder-led agencies, charging full fees, delivering 2-3 candidates not 20.

Rallee sits firmly in the second segment. The "premium recruiter" archetype has more space than 2022 because:
- Clients have been burned by commodity churn
- Senior candidates increasingly demand to be treated as people, not requisitions
- LinkedIn outreach saturation means cold templates don't work — agencies that genuinely know candidates win

## Pricing benchmarks (Q1 2026)

| Tier | Engagement model | Typical fee |
|---|---|---|
| Volume agency | Contingent, % of salary | 15-20% of first-year base |
| Mid-market specialist | Contingent or retained | 20-25% |
| Premium / boutique | Retained, multi-stage | 25-30% (some 33% for executive) |
| Executive search | Retained, exclusive | $40-80k per search or 33%+ |

Rallee's positioning supports the 25-30% retained tier on senior roles. Premium positioning unlocks pricing without volume.

## Categories with sustained demand

Strong in Q1 2026 across boutique recruiting:
- **Climate / sustainability roles** at scale-ups (incumbent ETS commitments driving hiring)
- **AI/ML engineering** at non-tech-native companies (banks, retailers, professional services)
- **Senior commercial roles** at growth-stage tech (CRO, VP Sales, Head of Partnerships)
- **Brand / marketing leadership** at premium consumer brands
- **People & Culture leaders** at scaling companies (Head of People, VP People)

Softer:
- Junior tech engineering (oversupply post-2024 layoffs)
- Generalist HR / TA (in-house teams covering this)
- Sales BDR roles (replaced by AI prospecting + senior closers)

## Where competitors are weak (and Rallee can press)

- **Cultural fit assessment.** Most agencies promise it; few do real diligence. Rallee's founder-led conversations are the moat.
- **Post-placement advocacy.** Once the fee is paid, most agencies disappear. Following up at 30/60/90 day is rare and remembered.
- **Talent pool curation.** Hot-talent newsletters and curated drops only work if the curator is trusted. Few are. Bree is one.
- **Editorial brand presence.** Recruiting websites are templates. A genuinely well-designed agency site converts at multiples of a Squarespace template.

## What's not working in the market

- LinkedIn cold InMail at scale (response rates collapsing — sub-1%)
- Generic "we have great candidates" outreach (clients have inbox fatigue)
- Volume submissions (clients want 2-3 right, not 20 mixed)
- Recruiter "thought leadership" content that's generic platitudes about "the war for talent"

## Implications for Rallee marketing

1. **Editorial brand IS the moat.** Continue investing in the bible, site, content quality. Don't dilute by chasing volume tactics.
2. **Hot-talent newsletter is the trojan horse.** Clients sign up for talent updates; relationship deepens from there.
3. **LinkedIn content should be observation-led, not motivational.** Specific takes on Australian recruitment market beat generic platitudes.
4. **Candidate-side content (CV templates, masterclasses) builds inbound talent pool.** Free entry point, builds Rallee as the agency that "gets it."
5. **Testimonials are under-leveraged.** Bree has many. Surface them prominently.
